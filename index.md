Privacy Policy for Magnifier
Last updated: 1 October 2026

This policy explains what Magnifier ("the app") does with your information. Magnifier is developed by APP2GO ("we", "us").

The short version: the app has no accounts and no sign-in, and we never see your photos or anything the camera reads. Those stay on your device. Google collects limited technical data to show ads, process subscriptions and report crashes, and — only where you have agreed to it — to tell us which parts of the app are being used. All of that is described in full below.

1. What stays on your device
The following never leaves your phone and is never sent to us or anyone else:

Camera images. The live camera view is processed entirely on your device. Frames are not uploaded, stored, or transmitted.
Recognized text. When you use the text recognition feature, the reading is performed by an on-device model that is included in the app itself. It works with no internet connection, and the text is never sent anywhere. It is shown to you on screen, and copied to your clipboard only when you press the copy button.
Photos you take. Photos are saved to your device's storage and to your system photo gallery. We have no access to them.
Your free-tier usage count. The app stores a small local record of how many text scans you have used in the past 24 hours, so it can apply the free-tier limit. It contains no identifying information and is deleted when you uninstall the app.
2. Data collected by third parties
Magnifier uses four Google services. Each collects data directly, under Google's own privacy policy.

Your consent comes first
In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and other regions where the law requires it, the app shows you a Google consent form the first time you open it. Advertising and usage analytics both wait for your answer. If you do not agree, usage analytics are not collected at all, and ads are limited accordingly.

You can change your mind at any time: open Settings in the app and choose Privacy options. Changes take effect the next time you open the app.

Where the law does not require a consent form, none is shown and the collection described below applies from first use.

Advertising — Google AdMob
Free users are shown ads. To serve and measure them, Google may collect:

your device's advertising identifier
device information (model, operating system version, language, screen size)
approximate location derived from your IP address
interactions with ads shown in the app
Google may use this to personalize ads. You can limit this in your device settings under Settings → Privacy → Ads, where you can reset or delete your advertising ID and opt out of ad personalization.

Google's advertising privacy information: https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads

Subscribers do not see ads, and no ad requests are made once a subscription is active.

Usage analytics — Google Analytics for Firebase
So that we can see which parts of the app people actually use, and where they get stuck, the app reports a small number of events to Google. Nothing is reported unless you have agreed to it, as described above.

The events are deliberately few, and this is all of them:

finishing the short introduction the first time you open the app
opening the magnifier
the first button you press after the magnifier opens
which feature you used — brightness, filters, taking a photo, text scanning, or the gallery
starting a subscription, and whether it began with a free trial
closing the subscription offer without buying, and whether you had got as far as Google's payment screen
Alongside those, Google collects:

a randomly generated app-installation identifier
your device's advertising identifier
device model, operating system version, app version, and language
the country your IP address resolves to
We see the result only as counts and charts — how many people did a thing, not who. It contains no photos, no recognized text, and nothing the camera has read. We cannot identify you from it, and we do not try to.

Firebase privacy information: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy

Crash reporting — Firebase Crashlytics
When the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, a report is sent so the problem can be fixed. A report contains:

the technical stack trace of the error
device model, operating system version and app version
a randomly generated installation identifier
Reports contain no photos, no recognized text, and nothing you have typed or read through the app.

Firebase privacy information: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy

Subscriptions — Google Play Billing
If you subscribe, the purchase is handled entirely by Google Play. We receive only confirmation that a valid subscription exists. We never see your name, payment card, or billing address.

Manage or cancel a subscription at any time through the Google Play Store app under Subscriptions.

3. Permissions the app requests
Permission	Why it is needed
Camera	The core function of the app — magnifying what the camera sees. Without it the app cannot work.
Photos / storage	To save the photos you choose to take, and to show them in the in-app gallery.
Flashlight	To turn on the torch when you tap the flashlight button.
The app does not request contacts, microphone, precise location, or access to files other than the photos it saves itself.

4. Children
Magnifier is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

5. Your rights
We hold no information that identifies you — the usage statistics described in section 2 reach us only as aggregate counts, with no way to trace them back to a person. There is therefore nothing for us to retrieve, correct, or delete on your behalf.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or a jurisdiction with comparable law (such as California), your rights of access, correction, deletion, and objection apply to the data handled by Google as described in section 2. Those requests should be directed to Google using the links above, or exercised through your device's privacy settings.

To stop usage analytics being collected, withdraw your consent through Settings → Privacy options in the app.

To remove everything the app has placed on your device, uninstall it. Photos you have already saved to your gallery remain there, and can be deleted like any other photo.
